Raunak Bhagate

I study applied AI and build ML systems that hold up once they're real. Most of that is inference. Not a new model, and not a bigger one. The work is the serving stack: throughput, latency, and what it costs to run when the hardware budget is fixed. A result that only looks good at unlimited scale is not a result. I care whether the system stays fast and stays reliable once it is actually serving.

I also work on agentic systems, as infrastructure rather than a demo. If a model can act, someone has to define what it can see, what it is allowed to do, and how you check that it did the right thing. That is a systems problem, the same family as serving: limits, cost, and proof.

Geospatial AI is the other thread. Labels are scarce, a convincing map is not a true one, and the output has to be a decision a person can use, not a model card.

Research and engineering are the same job here. The bar is whether it still works after it leaves the notebook.
